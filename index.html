Air Pollution in India: A Growing Concern

By: Environmental Correspondent | Published on: February 11, 2025

Air pollution in India has become a severe environmental issue, with 21 of the world's 30 most polluted cities located in the country as of 2019. Major contributors to this crisis include industrial emissions, vehicular pollution, construction dust, and the burning of biomass for cooking and heating.

According to studies, at least 140 million people in India breathe air that is over ten times the safe limit set by the World Health Organization. This alarming statistic underscores the urgent need for effective measures to combat air pollution.

"Air pollution in India is estimated to kill about 2 million people every year and is the fifth largest killer in the country."

Major Causes of Air Pollution

  • Industrial emissions
  • Vehicular pollution
  • Construction dust
  • Burning of biomass for cooking and heating
  • Crop residue burning in agricultural fields

Health Impacts

Prolonged exposure to polluted air can lead to respiratory diseases, cardiovascular problems, and reduced life expectancy. Non-smokers in India have been found to have 30% weaker lung function compared to Europeans, highlighting the detrimental health effects of poor air quality.

Government Initiatives

In response to the escalating crisis, the Indian government launched the National Clean Air Programme in 2019, aiming for a 20%-30% reduction in particulate matter concentrations by 2024. Additionally, projects like the Great Green Wall along the Aravalli range are being implemented to combat pollution and promote ecological balance.

Despite these efforts, continuous monitoring and stricter enforcement of pollution control measures are essential to ensure a healthier future for India's population.
